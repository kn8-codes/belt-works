# Brief for Janine: draft the next ShopFloor post

## Goal

Draft a public Belt.works blog post about ShopFloor.

This should complement the existing short ShopFloor page, not merely repeat it. The post should explain why ShopFloor needs to exist, what kind of local problem it solves, and what ethical lines it refuses to cross.

## Working title

Best option:

```text
ShopFloor: Who has the damn tool?
```

Other acceptable options:

```text
ShopFloor and the small problems that get expensive
A neighborhood repair layer for Akron
Tools, time, and the neighbor graph
```

## Core delimiter

Keep the post focused on one thing:

> ShopFloor as the local “who has the damn tool?” layer.

Do not try to explain the whole app, the whole roadmap, the whole Belt.works worldview, or the entire theory of mutual aid.

The post should answer this question:

> Why does Akron need a place to find tools, practical help, and local know-how?

That is the fence. Stay inside it.

## Thesis

Use this as the center of gravity:

> ShopFloor exists because a lot of community problems are not mysterious. They are logistical. Someone has the tool. Someone knows the fix. Someone needs help. The missing piece is a trustworthy way to connect those facts without turning every act of help into a transaction.

Everything in the post should serve that idea.

## Tone

Match the Belt.works/A11y post tone:

- Plainspoken.
- Akron-first.
- Concrete before abstract.
- Moral without being preachy.
- Dry where appropriate.
- No startup announcement voice.
- No AI slop.
- No “revolutionizing community infrastructure” nonsense. We are adults here.

Useful tonal examples:

```text
Akron knows what a locked door costs.
```

```text
At belt.works, we don’t do locked doors.
```

ShopFloor should feel related to that voice, but more practical, garage/basement/porch/workbench than curb-cut/civic access.

## What to include

### 1. Open with a concrete small problem

Start with a real situation, not an abstract explanation.

Possible opening directions:

```text
You need a ladder for twenty minutes.

Not enough to buy one. Not worth renting a truck. Not the kind of problem that should turn into a $180 service call. Just a ladder, a neighbor, and maybe somebody who knows how to not fall off the damn thing.
```

Or:

```text
A lot of local problems are small until you’re the one stuck with them.

A busted cabinet hinge. A dead outlet. A couch that needs moved. A tool you need once and can’t justify buying.
```

The point: ShopFloor lives in the world of small practical problems that get expensive when nobody knows who can help.

### 2. Name the broken system

Describe what happens now:

- people buy tools they only use once;
- tools sit in garages unused;
- people need help but do not know who to ask;
- skilled neighbors are invisible unless you already know them;
- Facebook groups are chaotic;
- paid services are too expensive for small problems;
- mutual aid often lacks operational structure.

Do not dunk on people. Dunk on the friction.

### 3. Explain the core idea

Include this sentence or a close variant:

```text
ShopFloor is a neighborhood repair time bank for tools, practical help, and local know-how.
```

Then unpack the core objects:

- tools: what people have;
- skills: what people know how to do;
- requests: what people need;
- availability: when help is realistic;
- neighborhoods: because distance matters;
- time ledger: contribution visible without turning help into gig work.

### 4. Center “who has the damn tool?”

This phrase should be central.

Possible hinge paragraph:

```text
The first question is often not “Who can solve my life?”
It is “Who has the damn tool?”
```

Then expand:

```text
Once you know who has the tool, the next question is who knows how to use it, who can lend a hand, and what kind of help keeps the situation from becoming another expensive mess.
```

### 5. Say what ShopFloor is not

This protects the soul of the thing.

Say plainly that ShopFloor is not:

- Angi;
- TaskRabbit;
- a gig marketplace;
- charity theater;
- a social feed;
- another app trying to “disrupt” neighbors helping neighbors.

Possible line:

```text
ShopFloor is not trying to turn every neighbor into a vendor. We have enough systems that make people monetize their exhaustion.
```

### 6. Explain time banking lightly

Do not write a full history of time banking. Keep it practical.

Possible line:

```text
Money is scarce. Time is unevenly distributed. Skill is everywhere, but badly indexed.
```

Explain that the ledger is:

- not a currency scam;
- not perfect accounting;
- not a moral score;
- just a way to make contribution visible;
- a way to reduce the “same three people do everything” problem.

### 7. Explain why Akron first

Connect it to place.

Akron has:

- old houses;
- garages;
- porches;
- basements;
- informal skill networks;
- people who know how to fix things;
- people who need help before small problems become expensive.

Possible line:

```text
Akron already has the raw material for this: old houses, borrowed tools, people who can fix things, and people who have learned not to ask for help until the problem is already expensive.
```

### 8. Mention sustainability, but keep it brief

Use this line:

```text
ShopFloor is free to use because barriers to help are bugs.
```

Then one short paragraph:

```text
The work still has to survive. Hosting, maintenance, moderation, accessibility, and support cost money. That ask belongs on belt.works, not between someone and the help they need.
```

Do not turn this post into the support page. The support page already handles the money ask.

### 9. End with honest alpha language

Do not pretend ShopFloor is done.

Say:

- it is early;
- it starts in Akron;
- it needs friendly testers soon;
- it should prove itself with small useful exchanges.

Possible closer:

```text
The first version does not need to solve every civic failure in Summit County. It needs to help one person find one tool, one neighbor get one repair done, and one small problem stay small.
```

## What not to include

Do not include:

- full feature roadmap;
- Supabase/auth/database details;
- deployment plans;
- moderation architecture;
- a long history of time banking;
- a big monetization explanation;
- a lecture on mutual aid;
- anything centered on AI or the agent mesh;
- fake launch energy.

This is not a launch announcement. It is a clear statement of what we are building and why.

Avoid lines that sound like:

```text
Announcing the future of community repair infrastructure.
```

That smells like venture capital wearing a thrift-store jacket.
